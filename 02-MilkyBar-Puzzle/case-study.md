# Milkybar — When the Product Became the Game

## Product Decisions That Made Money · #02

### The Question

What if chocolate wasn't just something you ate?

What if the product itself became part of the experience?

---

## The Product Decision

Milkybar built on its existing territory of imagination, curiosity, learning and play.

The interactive moulds gave that idea a physical, tactile experience — turning the chocolate into something children could look at, explore and interact with, rather than simply consume. VML describes the moulds as giving the brand's message a "tactile purpose." :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

The experience becomes:

**Open → Explore → Play → Remember**

---

## Why It Matters

This is a small but interesting product-thinking shift:

**Don't only design what people consume.  
Design what they experience.**

The product itself becomes part of the brand experience.

For Milkybar, this fits directly with its positioning around imagination and "Play, Eat & Learn." :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

## The Business Connection

VML describes the challenge as strengthening Milkybar's relevance with children while maintaining its association with imagination, play and learning. The agency identifies relevance and growth as the broader business opportunity. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Nestlé's campaign messaging similarly focused on imagination, learning and curiosity. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

### What we can prove

- Interactive moulds were part of the Milkybar experience.
- They were designed to give the brand's imagination/learning proposition a tactile expression.
- The broader strategic objective included improving brand relevance and supporting growth. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

### What we should NOT claim

There is no reliable public source identified for this case study that attributes a specific revenue increase directly to the puzzle/mould experience.

So this case study intentionally does **not** invent a revenue figure.

---

## PM Takeaway

A product doesn't always need another feature.

Sometimes the opportunity is to change what the user can **do with the product**.

> **Don't only design what people consume.  
> Design what they experience.**
